# Loan and Investment as first-class entities, not Account types

`CONTEXT.md` and the `accounts` table originally modelled Cash, Bank, Credit Card, Investment, and Loan as five variants of one `AccountType` enum on a single `Accounts` row — a name, a Unit, a starting balance, nothing type-specific. Designing Loan and Investment's actual requirements (`docs/product-requirements.md` §1.4's "Personal Investors" and "Personal Loan" personas, still future-consideration with no FRs defined) surfaced data neither Account nor a plain Transaction can express: a Loan needs principal and an interest rate tracked as a *history* over time, not a single fixed value — Australian loans are commonly variable-rate, so a scalar rate field would be wrong from day one. An Investment needs a quantity that changes only through a buy/sell action, never through an ordinary Transaction. Both personas also specifically asked for their own workflow, reporting, and view, not a filtered slice of the generic Accounts screen.

We considered keeping Loan and Investment as `AccountType` variants and bolting the extra data on as 1:1 satellite tables (a `loans` table keyed by `account_id`, carrying principal/rate-history/term; an `investments` table the same way) — the minimal-diff option, leaving the existing `Accounts` table, its CRUD, and every Account-uniform TUI screen untouched. We rejected it: a satellite table solves the *data* gap (rate history, quantity) but not the *view* gap — Loan and Investment would still render inside the same Accounts list, grouped by `AccountType`, which is exactly what the "own workflow, reporting, and view" requirement asked to move away from. Solving only the data problem while leaving the presentation problem in place would have paid most of this decision's cost for less than its full benefit.

Instead, Loan and Investment become fully separate primary entities, not kinds of Account. `Account` narrows to Cash, Bank, and Credit Card — the three variants that already share one shape (Balance computed from Transactions, nothing type-specific) — and keeps its own name and its own screen. `Loan` and `Investment` each get their own table, their own record shape, and (eventually) their own view; the app may still group all three under one "Accounts" navigation area for browsing, but that's a UI grouping, not a shared entity. `Institution` — previously explicitly *not* modelled in V1 (`CONTEXT.md`, prior wording) — is promoted alongside them to a first-class entity, with a **mandatory** link from every Account, Loan, and Investment. Two weaker options were rejected: an optional (nullable) link, which invites inconsistent data for real-world Bank/Loan/Investment records that always have a genuine institution; and forcing a real Institution choice even on a Cash Account, which is meaningless for physical cash. Cash instead links to a system-seeded placeholder Institution row, keeping the FK non-null everywhere at the cost of one synthetic row.

Pulling Loan and Investment out from under Account also removes the thing that let a Loan repayment or an Investment trade be recorded as "just a Transaction" — Transactions post against an Account, and Loan/Investment no longer are one. Two new cross-entity mechanisms fill that gap, deliberately kept separate from each other rather than unified into one generic "transfer" concept, because their mechanics genuinely differ: **Trade** (`Account` ↔ `Investment`) is quantity- and price-aware and is the sole place this codebase crosses Units without an exchange rate — each side stays denominated in its own Unit, the Trade's price is what ties the two amounts together, and this does not reopen the "Personal Ledger doesn't convert between Units in V1" rule (`CONTEXT.md`, Unit). **Repayment** (`Account` ↔ `Loan`) instead reuses the same-Account **Split** concept for its principal/interest breakdown. Split itself is a smaller, separable change worth noting: every Transaction is now Date + Account + one-or-more Splits (Category, Amount, optional Payee, optional Tags), with a plain single-Category Transaction just being the one-Split case — this does not reopen [ADR-0001](0001-single-entry-not-double-entry.md)'s single-entry-not-double-entry decision, since a Split still stays within one Account and one row per line, not a balanced debit/credit pair across Accounts.

This is design/vocabulary scope only — `CONTEXT.md` and `docs/accounts.md` record the resulting shape in full — and does not move Loan or Investment tracking out of "future consideration" in `docs/product-requirements.md`. No migration, table, or code exists for any of Institution, Loan, Investment, Split, Trade, or Repayment yet; this ADR records why the shape is what it is before that build work starts, so a future implementer doesn't "simplify" Loan back into an `AccountType` variant without knowing why it was deliberately pulled out.
