# Account as a formal umbrella over four Account Kinds, including a new Credit Card Account

[ADR-0017](0017-loan-and-investment-as-entities-not-account-types.md) promoted Loan and Investment out of `Account` into their own primary entities, narrowing `Account` to Cash/Bank/Credit Card, but left the shared concept behind those three (now four) separate entities unnamed — `CONTEXT.md` only said the app "may still group Account, Loan, and Investment together under one 'Accounts' area of navigation," an informal UI grouping, not a defined term. Revisiting the wording surfaced that gap directly: describing "every account" as "nominated as one of three types" read as either a pure rename or a reversal of ADR-0017 back to one shared table with a type column, and those have very different consequences. We resolved it as a formal rename, not a reversal: `Account` becomes a real glossary term — "a place where value is held or owed" — and `Account Kind` names which of several separate entities a given Account is. The underlying structure from ADR-0017 is unchanged: Transaction Account, Credit Card Account, Loan Account, and Investment Account are still four separate record shapes, not four values of one type column on one table. `Account` (renamed from the ADR-0017 narrow sense) becomes `Transaction Account`, `Loan` becomes `Loan Account`, and `Investment` becomes `Investment Account`, so every Account Kind's name is now unambiguous on its own.

Formalising "Account Kind" exposed a genuine naming collision: the prior `Account` entry used "Account Type" for its Cash/Bank/Credit-Card classification, and `lib-core::AccountType` is the actual Rust enum name for it today. Once `Account` became an umbrella over several Kinds, "Account Type" was ambiguous between *which Kind an Account is* and *the Cash/Bank sub-classification within Transaction Account specifically*. We split these into two deliberately different names — `Account Kind` for the former, `Transaction Account Type` for the latter — so a reader can tell which axis is meant from the term alone, without more context. This mirrors why ADR-0017 exists at all: leaving an ambiguous term in place invites exactly the kind of drift these ADRs are written to prevent.

Credit Card is promoted out of Transaction Account Type into its own, fourth Account Kind — `Credit Card Account` — carrying data neither Transaction Account nor Loan Account has: a Credit Limit and an interest rate tracked as a history over time (the same rate-history shape ADR-0017 gave Loan, since card rates are commonly variable too), plus a statement/due date. Unlike Loan Account, though, Credit Card Account keeps the Transaction/Split mechanics of Transaction Account: it accepts Transactions directly, and its Balance is computed from them the same way. We considered giving Credit Card the full Loan-Account treatment — no direct Transactions, spending and repayment both routed through dedicated mechanisms — and rejected it: real-world card spending genuinely is a plain, Categorised Transaction (swipe the card, buy groceries), unlike a Loan, which never accepts a Transaction at all. Forcing Credit Card spending through a Trade/Repayment-style mechanism would model against the grain of how the data actually looks, for no benefit — the only things Credit Card actually needs beyond Transaction Account are the extra fields and its own view, not new value-movement machinery. Consequently, **paying down a Credit Card Account is an ordinary same-Unit Transaction** (the existing "a Transaction may move to another Account sharing that same Unit" rule already covers it) — not the Repayment mechanism, which stays specific to Loan Account precisely because Loan Account, unlike Credit Card Account, cannot accept a Transaction at all.

This is design/vocabulary scope only, same as ADR-0017 — `CONTEXT.md` and `docs/accounts.md` record the resulting shape in full, including a comparison table of which Account Kinds accept Transactions directly, have a Balance, and what their "current amount" concept is called (Balance, Balance, Outstanding Principal, Quantity, for Transaction/Credit-Card/Loan/Investment Account respectively). No migration, table, or code exists yet for Institution, Credit Card Account, Loan Account, Investment Account, Split, Trade, or Repayment; this ADR, alongside ADR-0017, records why the shape is what it is before that build work starts.
